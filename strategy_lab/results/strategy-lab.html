The one positive number, VWAP pullback short on NSE (+0.11R on the check days), lost −0.20R on the pick days first. The check days were a falling month, which favours short trades. That is the market's direction, not an edge, and it needs about ₹10 lakh of capital per trade.

Order block rules tested

  1. Swing high: a bar higher than the two bars either side.
  2. Break of structure: a close above the latest swing high.
  3. Order block: the last down candle before that up-move; the zone runs from its high (or body) to its low.
  4. Optional: the move must leave a fair value gap.
  5. Buy with a limit order when price comes back into the zone; stop just under the zone; target 1R, 2R or the move's high.

Shorts use the mirror image. One trade per stock per day; out by the bot's flat-by time.

VWAP pullback rules tested

  1. VWAP: the day's volume-weighted average price, from the open.
  2. Trend: the last 3 or 6 bars closed above a rising VWAP.
  3. Signal: a green bar that dips to VWAP and closes back above it.
  4. Buy at that bar's close; stop under the dip bar or 1 ATR under VWAP; target 1R, 1.5R or 2R.

Shorts use the mirror image. Both strategies were also tested with and without the bots' 20-day trend filter.

Limits of this test

  • Free 5-minute stock data only goes back 60 days, so each stock period is about 2–3 months; crypto uses 4 months of 15-minute bars.
  • There is no single agreed definition of an order block. This is a common Smart Money Concepts version; other versions could differ, but a near-zero result before charges suggests the core idea adds little.
  • Crypto INR order blocks were positive before charges (+0.24R) on 99 trades, but the same rule on USDT markets was not, and the 0.59% fee per side wipes it out either way.
  • Short selling was tested for stocks only. CoinDCX spot does not allow it.
